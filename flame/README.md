## Historical Context: Doctrine of Signatures

The Doctrine of Signatures (Latin: "signatura rerum") is a hermetic philosophy developed by Paracelsus (1493-1541) in his alchemical texts. The doctrine posits that God marked plants and minerals with visual signatures resembling the ailments they can cure, embodying the principle "as above, so below."

### Key Paracelsus Texts

- **Paramirum** (1531) - Foundation of medical philosophy
- **De Natura Rerum** (1537) - Nature and transformation of substances
- **De Viribus Membrorum** (1530) - Powers of body members
- **Archidoxis Magica** (1530) - Magical medicine and elixir creation

### Examples from Alchemical Texts

1. **Walnuts for Brain Ailments** - Shell resembles skull/brain; used for head wounds and memory issues
2. **Lungwort for Lung Diseases** - Leaves spotted like lungs; prescribed for pulmonary issues
3. **Liverwort for Liver Problems** - Liver-shaped leaves; treated jaundice and liver ailments
4. **Eyebright for Eye Conditions** - Eye-like flowers; cured eye inflammation
5. **Heart's Ease (Viola tricolor) for Heart Issues** - Heart-shaped leaves; eased heart pains
6. **Maidenhair Fern for Hair Loss** - Hair-like fronds; promoted hair growth
